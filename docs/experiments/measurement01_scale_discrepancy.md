# Why the new ruler reconstruction still had a large size error

The approximately **9.71× size gap is in the inertial scale estimate**. The new
pairwise reconstruction's held-out ruler intervals already agree with a separate
5 cm reference to within 1.66%. A controlled orientation-source substitution
removes most of the large gap, without changing matches, K, poses, ruler
annotations, integration equations or the scale/initial-velocity solver.

## Reproduce

```bash
.venv/bin/python tools/validation/explain_scale_discrepancy.py
```

This reads the existing `ruler_retry/ruler_retry.json` and its `scene.json`,
verifies the recorded input hashes, reproduces all six original inertial scales,
and writes to ignored `data/processed/measurement01/scale_discrepancy/`:

- `scale_discrepancy.json`: orientation increments, scale decomposition, gyro
  trials, 41-point timing sweep, acceleration-error families and source hashes.
- `scale_discrepancy.png`: motion-vector directions, timing sensitivity,
  scale/bias ambiguity and camera-baseline comparisons.

Raw data, original M4/M7 artifacts and the retry scene remain unchanged. No
candidate metric scale is applied. The new matcher has its existing interactive
review at `matching_review/review.html`; the original M4 rectified image is an
older diagnostic, not an image regenerated by the new matching pipeline.

## 1. The small number is a projection of inconsistent motion vectors

The ruler reference needs **0.254862 m per reconstruction unit**. The original
rotation-vector-based inertial fit returns **0.026259 m/unit**, making a 5 cm
interval approximately **0.515 cm**. For example, the unchanged visual 1–2
baseline becomes 25.40 cm under the ruler reference, but 2.617 cm under that
inertial candidate. Different arbitrary visual gauges do not explain this gap:
rescaling visual coordinates changes both scale factors inversely.

For the two elapsed times `t1,t2`, eliminate unknown initial velocity:

```text
q_visual = (C2-C0)/t2 - (C1-C0)/t1
q_imu    = D2/t2 - D1/t1
s        = dot(q_visual,q_imu) / dot(q_visual,q_visual)
         = norm(q_imu)/norm(q_visual) * cos(angle)
```

`D` is the zero-initial-velocity double acceleration integral. These are
velocity-difference vectors, not camera displacement vectors or raw acceleration.
For button timing and nominal gravity, their angle is **83.175°**. Their magnitude
ratio is **0.220974 m/unit**, already near the ruler reference; multiplying by the
small cosine gives **0.026259 m/unit**. The solver shrinks the scale because the
directions disagree. A centimetre/metre conversion cannot repair that mismatch.

## 2. Recorded fused orientation contains jumps unsupported by the gyro

At **4.122458–4.132536 s**, the recorded rotation vector changes by **5.543°**,
while gyro integration over the same interval gives only **0.0544°**. The jump's
axis lies within **0.364° of world vertical**. This is consistent with a fused
heading correction; the recording does not establish its cause. It is not
evidence that the phone physically rotated by 5.5° or that gravity tilted by
5.5°. The jump is present despite recorded sensor accuracy 3.

As a declared sensitivity trial, seed orientation once from the interpolated
rotation vector at the first covered gyro timestamp, then integrate the
device-frame gyro with zero assumed gyro bias. Rotate acceleration using this trajectory and rerun the
same scale fit. The ruler is used only afterward for comparison:

| Timing hypothesis | Fused orientation, nominal gravity | Gyro orientation, nominal gravity | Gyro orientation, early gravity/bias proxy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Button request | 0.515 cm | **4.949 cm** | 4.094 cm |
| Request/callback midpoint | 0.227 cm | **4.788 cm** | 3.922 cm |
| JPEG callback | Negative scale, rejected | 4.330 cm | 3.442 cm |

Values are the reconstructed **5–10 cm interval**, whose actual length is 5 cm.
The gyro/button/nominal trial independently estimates **0.252238 m/unit**, giving
all three marked intervals **4.949, 4.975 and 4.947 cm** (maximum error 1.064%).
Its visual-versus-inertial relative rotation errors also fall from
**8.25°, 8.77°, 2.51°** to **1.18°, 0.85°, 1.61°** for pairs 0–1, 0–2, 1–2.

This controlled substitution strongly implicates orientation handling in the
large error. It changes the whole orientation trajectory, including tilt and
heading; it does not isolate the contribution of the single heading jump.
Gyro integration is not a calibrated orientation ground truth.

## 3. Good agreement in one trial is insufficient to accept metric scale

- **Gravity/bias:** a constant world acceleration error of just **0.1101 m/s²**
  (1.123% of gravity) reconciles the original fused-orientation trajectory with
  the ruler reference. This is a diagnostic error hypothesis, not an estimated
  sensor bias. The early rotated median differs from nominal gravity by
  0.0836 m/s², a comparable magnitude, but that window is not certified stationary.
  Using the proxy does not consistently improve size accuracy.
- **Timing:** JPEG callback delays are **0.613, 0.524 and 0.378 s**. There are
  no exposure timestamps. Even the gyro/nominal trial changes from 4.949 to
  4.330 cm between request and callback assumptions, crossing the 10% limit.
- **Residuals:** the gyro/button/nominal trial still has **9.40 cm per-coordinate
  position residual RMS**, despite the nearly correct ruler scale. The early proxy
  reduces that residual to 0.333 cm but produces an approximately 18% size error.
  A small residual alone also cannot certify scale.
- **Observability:** three poses give six scalar position equations. Scale,
  initial velocity and a free constant world acceleration error have seven
  unknowns. Zero scale, the inertial candidate and the ruler-reference scale
  each fit those positions exactly with different error/velocity hypotheses.
  The diagnostic exports this continuum rather than selecting a bias to pass
  the ruler gate. A true constant device bias needs rotation-dependent terms
  and remains uncalibrated as well.
- **Visual calibration:** K is still metadata-derived, distortion is unknown,
  and camera/IMU mounting and lever arm remain assumptions. The local ruler
  shape check does not certify global scene accuracy.

The next useful changes are actual exposure timestamps and validated stationary
sensor intervals, followed by bias-aware gyro/accelerometer processing and more
than three constrained visual positions. Simply increasing match count or
changing length units does not resolve the remaining uncertainty. The existing
10% gate and unaccepted metric-scale status remain in force.
