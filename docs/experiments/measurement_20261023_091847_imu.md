## What is being compared

There is no recorded Android linear-acceleration stream. The recorded phone
vectors describe **orientation**, in quaternion xyzw form. The comparison uses
the same measured accelerometer vector with three orientation estimates:

1. Gyro integration, seeded once by the first common regular rotation-vector
   orientation, with no additional gyro bias fit when rest checks fail.
2. Android's regular rotation vector, including compass fusion.
3. Android's game rotation vector, aligned once about world vertical to the
   regular vector's initial heading. Its arbitrary heading is not interpreted
   as north, and its tilt is not aligned away.

Common coverage is **0.065071–28.439603 s**, containing **2816 accelerometer
samples**. Quaternion interpolation uses the original sensor timestamps.
Gyro integration includes the exact common endpoints. Native quaternion
increments are compared against gyro integration over the same native interval,
not against a nearby instantaneous angular rate.

With `R` mapping device coordinates to the shared world reference:

```text
predicted_gravity_device = R.T * [0,0,9.80665]
estimated_linear_device = measured_acceleration_device - predicted_gravity_device
estimated_linear_world  = R * measured_acceleration_device - [0,0,9.80665]
```

The gravity figure compares measured acceleration with predicted device gravity.
The acceleration figure compares the world-frame residuals. Residuals contain
actual phone acceleration as well as sensor/orientation error; they are not
assumed zero while the phone moves. Lecture 002 **pp. 36–45 and 81–83** supplies
the orthonormal reference changes; sensor fusion/gravity processing extends it.

## Results

Differences below use gyro integration as a comparison baseline, **not ground
truth**. RMS is the root mean squared 3D vector norm, not per-axis RMS.

| Phone orientation source | Maximum 3D orientation difference | Maximum tilt difference | Acceleration difference RMS | Maximum acceleration difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Regular rotation vector | 8.807° | 3.303° | 0.2014 m/s² | 0.6000 m/s² |
| Game rotation vector | 3.303° | 3.303° | 0.1970 m/s² | 0.5748 m/s² |

The regular vector's largest native increment disagreement occurs at
**9.617182–9.627258 s**: it reports **2.820°** rotation while gyro integration
reports **0.223°**. The rotations disagree by **3.013°**; the recorded rotation
axis is within **1.905° of world vertical**. There are four native increments with
more than one degree of disagreement. This is consistent with fused heading
corrections; it does not establish their physical cause.

The game vector's largest native increment disagreement is **0.217°**, with no
increments over one degree. It lacks the regular vector's large abrupt heading
changes in this capture. Its constant initial heading adjustment is **14.919°**.

The two phone vectors' tilt estimates agree within **0.000738°**, after resampling
to the same accelerometer timestamps. Their acceleration estimates differ by
**0.03234 m/s² RMS**. Most of their disagreement with gyro-integrated acceleration
therefore comes from their shared tilt disagreement with the gyro trajectory,
not from the additional compass-heading corrections alone. A single unchanging
heading alignment has no effect on their gravity direction.

This adds a qualification to the previous recording: removing abrupt compass
updates addresses heading jumps, but does not establish gyro-only tilt as more
accurate. The gyro may drift, and phone fusion may react to real acceleration.
There is no independent orientation/acceleration reference in this comparison
to distinguish those explanations. Images have not been reconstructed here.

## Capture quality and limitations

The declared **0.1–1.6 s** rest window fails the existing stationary gates:
maximum per-axis acceleration standard deviation is **0.1869 m/s²** (limit 0.05),
and gyro standard deviation is **0.04265 rad/s** (limit 0.005). Gyro bias remains
unestimated. An exploratory scan of 1.5-second windows, at 0.25-second steps,
also found no interval passing these unchanged gates. This does not certify a
particular source as wrong or justify fitting a bias to improve agreement.

Compass magnitude spans **44.949–48.286 microtesla**, median **46.753**, with
Android accuracy status 2. A plausible magnitude does not prove undisturbed
magnetic heading. The raw compass is retained for later reference validation;
it is not used to repeatedly correct this gyro comparison.

Six images use their own REALTIME camera frame timestamps as primary time.
Images **2 and 6** retain button time because their camera timestamps precede
the request by **1.977 and 9.850 ms**, respectively. This is the current app's
strict fallback policy, not evidence that these retained frame timestamps are
necessarily incorrect. Request-to-callback latency ranges from **0.336 to
1.363 s**; image 1's frame is about 1.030 s after its button request. Using actual
frame timing therefore matters. The comparison does not rewrite any timestamp.

Source capture hashes remain unchanged. No camera pose, overlap association,
reconstruction or metric scale is fitted or changed. The existing measurement
acceptance gate remains in force.
